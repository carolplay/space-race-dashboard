# Demand scenarios, 2026–2036

Generated artifact: `data/metrics/demand-forecast.json`; reproduce with `npm run data:update:forecast`. The update scripts for GCAT history and ITU filings regenerate it after changing their source files.

## What the curves mean

- Global orbital launch attempts: 2020–2025 are GCAT LaunchLog observations. 2026 is a full-year *nowcast*: 2025 full-year attempts in each GCAT region multiplied by the 2026/2025 ratio through the matched September 24 cutoffs. For 2027–2036, add the 2023–2025 mean annual increase, multiplied by 0.85 each subsequent year. This is an illustrative momentum scenario, not committed launch demand, a capacity forecast, or a future mission manifest.
- Orbital payload stock: 2016–2025 are GCAT year-end counts, including inactive payloads; 2026 is the September 24 snapshot. Project future net additions from 40% of the 2024–2025 full-year stock gain and 60% of the annualized 2025–2026-to-date gain. Dampen the increment by 12% each year. These counts are not “active operational satellites” and are not mass in orbit.
- Five fleet curves: observed current estimates come from GCAT-derived constellation data. The recent annualized net pace uses the latest pre-cutoff snapshot at least 365 days earlier. Future annual increments decay by 12% per year. That deliberately ignores announced goals, end-of-life retirements, regulatory eligibility and capacity changes, so use only as a no-step-change comparator.
- Selected on-time target pressure: Amazon Leo's FCC 2029 Gen1 target, Qianfan's commercial 2027 and 2030 goals, and GW-A59's 2029/2032/2034 milestones *if* the relevant RES35 rule applies. A straight line from present brand-level inventory to each target estimates annual additional satellites. Present brand-level satellites are optimistically credited even though their eligibility for a particular filing or generation is unverified. The conversion to launch equivalents uses assumed 20, 40 or 80 satellites per launch. Mass, vehicle lift, orbit, replacements, launch failures and other missions are omitted. Thus this is a sensitivity illustration, **not a verified minimum requirement**. It overlaps the global launch trend and must never be added to it.
- CTC-1 stress test: [ITU public notice](https://www.itu.int/itu-r/space/apps/public/spaceexplorer/networks-explorer/space-stations/dashboard/non-plans/CTC-1/125520195) reports 96,714 and the 2032-12-29 earliest BIU limit. The 2034/2037/2039 10/50/100% figures assume the *entire* notice persists and [RES35](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/Pages/res35main.aspx) applies after that limit. This is not established. Its orbit range extends far beyond LEO; do not convert the filing to launch equivalents or add it to physical stock. CTC-2 is also excluded, rather than counting related notices twice.

## Evidence boundaries

The [ITU FAQ](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/Pages/FAQspace.aspx) distinguishes per-frequency-group regulatory periods, bringing into use, and subsequent deployment milestones. The [ITU RES35 page](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/space/Pages/res35main.aspx) describes the scope and reporting stages. FCC authorization clocks remain separate. The original official source URLs and source cutoff are embedded in `demand-forecast.json`; inspect those before changing target assumptions. A new filing does not certify an operating constellation, assign all satellites to one commercial brand, or prove the applicability of RES35 to every frequency group.
